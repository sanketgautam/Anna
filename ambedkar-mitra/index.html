1891 — 1956

A life that
became a path

From a schoolboy made to sit apart, to the chief architect of the world's longest written constitution. In the app, this journey is not a list of dates — you walk it with him, place by place, and pause wherever your own life meets his.

“Cultivation of mind should be the ultimate aim of human existence.”

— Annihilation of Caste, 1936
  1. 1891

    Born in Mhow

    Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar is born on 14 April into a Mahar family — a community then treated as “untouchable” and kept from schools, wells and temples.

  2. 1912

    A degree, against the odds

    He graduates from Elphinstone College, Bombay — among the very first from his community to earn a degree — helped by a scholarship from the Maharaja of Baroda.

  3. 1913–16

    Columbia University, New York

    Economics, politics, philosophy. He reads voraciously and begins to see India's caste question with the tools of scholarship.

  4. 1923

    Doctor, barrister — and back home

    A DSc from the London School of Economics and called to the Bar, he returns not to comfort, but to organise: journals, hostels and associations for the oppressed.

  5. 1927

    Mahad: a drink of water

    At the Chavdar tank in Mahad, he leads thousands to claim the simple human right to drink public water. Later that year, the Manusmriti is publicly burned.

  6. 1936

    Annihilation of Caste

    His undelivered presidential address becomes one of India's most argued-with books: caste, he writes, is “a notion, a state of the mind” — and minds can change.

  7. 1947

    First Law Minister & Drafting Chairman

    Independent India asks him to chair the committee drafting its Constitution — the boy kept out of the classroom now writes the rules of the republic.

  8. 1949

    The Constitution is adopted

    On 26 November 1949 the Constitution is adopted: liberty, equality, fraternity and justice — secured, at least in law, for every Indian.

  9. 1956

    Nagpur, and farewell

    On 14 October, at what is now Deekshabhoomi, Nagpur, he embraces Buddhism with lakhs of followers, seeking a path of equality and compassion. He passes away on 6 December in Delhi.

Honest answers

Questions you
may be asking

Is this really Dr Ambedkar?

No — and we will never pretend otherwise. Ambedkar Mitra is a live voice and video AI interpretation of Dr B. R. Ambedkar's ideas. You speak, it listens, and it answers for conversation, advice and life lessons drawn from his writings and thought. It is a study companion and a mentor in spirit: patient, plain-spoken, and always available. It is an interpretation, not the historical person.

Where do the answers come from?

From his books, speeches and the historical record — Annihilation of Caste, The Buddha and His Dhamma, the collected Writings and Speeches, and the Constitution he drafted. Answers in the app carry their source, so you can always go and read the original. If we cannot source it, the companion should say so.

Which languages can I speak?

Starting with Hindi, English and Marathi — the languages spoken at home by the community this is built for first. More Indian languages will follow, shaped by what waitlist members ask for.

I don't like talking to apps. Is there anything for me?

Yes. The Stories feed is a quiet, scrollable reel of one-minute stories from his life — no talking required. Read one with your morning chai; share the ones that stay with you.

Can our school, museum or study circle use it?

That is exactly who it is being built with. Join the waitlist and mention your institution in your email — early partners will help shape the classroom and exhibit modes.

When does it launch, and what will it cost to listen?

Soon — the waitlist hears first. The goal is a free-to-listen core, built for an ordinary smartphone, because guidance on rights, study and daily life should be within everyone's reach.
